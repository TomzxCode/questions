# How do I fix team misalignment, unclear responsibilities, and people stepping on each other's toes?

Team members make decisions without consulting anyone, roles and ownership are unclear, and there's little coordination. It looks like a collaboration problem.

## Answer

The situation is three problems wrapped together, each with a known fix:

1. **No ownership boundaries** - people don't know what's theirs to decide, so they overstep or wait. Fix: map ownership explicitly (a Team API / Team Topologies approach: who owns what, inputs/outputs, interaction modes).
2. **No decision-making norms** - decisions get made silently because there's no agreed process for what needs consultation vs. what's autonomous. Fix: a team charter spelling out decision rights (e.g., RACI) and operating norms.
3. **No shared target** - without common goals, everyone optimizes locally. Fix: shared goals/OKRs the team co-owns.

The fastest leverage is the charter + ownership map together, because they answer the two questions the team can't currently answer: "who owns what" and "how do we decide."

### Practical advice, in order of leverage

**Start with decision rights, not org charts.** Agree on one rule: if a change affects another person's area, check with them first. Then enforce it by asking "did you run this past X?" each time it doesn't happen. Norms are built by repetition, not by writing them down once.

**Make ownership visible in 30 minutes.** Whiteboard the product broken into areas, with a name next to each. Overlaps and gaps surface themselves. People step on toes mostly because boundaries are genuinely unclear, not out of carelessness.

**Check whether everyone knows the current top priority.** Misalignment is often an information problem, not a values problem. One shared, visible priority list beats any framework.

**Model the behavior you want.** You can't mandate collaboration, you demonstrate it. When deciding something that touches others, say out loud "I'm checking with X first" and explain why.

**Have the meta-conversation.** Tell the team what you're seeing and ask if they see it too. Naming the problem collectively is a prerequisite to solving it collectively.

**Avoid big process rollouts.** Small, visible behavior changes stick. A heavyweight "collaboration framework" usually gets ignored.

### Artifacts that can help later

- Team charter (purpose, roles, decision rights, operating norms, success metrics)
- Team API (ownership boundaries, dependencies, inputs/outputs, interaction modes)
- Shared goals / OKRs
